Reading Response 7.

The Coming Age of Calm Technology

Response07 / 07
SourceWeiser & Brown, 1996 ↗

This is a reading response to The Coming Age of Calm Technology, Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown’s argument that as computers spread into everything around us, they need to stay calm and out of the way.

Reading this in 2026 felt strange, because their prediction that computers would be everywhere came true, but the calm part didn’t. The Dangling String just twitches quietly in the corner of a hallway until you choose to notice it, while my phone does the opposite: its constant, repetitive notifications and red badges decide for me what deserves my attention right now. I also wondered whose calm these designs actually protect. Calm technology can be a double-edged sword. A window into someone’s office, or a “last active” status, can feel calm to the person looking but stressful to the person being seen.

As a design student, I liked their small point that font and layout quietly guide a reader’s mind. Typography already creates that kind of calm design: people rarely look at it directly, but it keeps them oriented.
