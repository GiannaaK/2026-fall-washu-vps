Lost in Paris.

Writing Exercise #3

Prompt

The Slow Way

When do you cherish the slow or hard way of doing something? Why?

The moment I read this question, I thought of a trip to Paris with my friend. Our mobile data barely worked throughout the trip, which meant we couldn't rely on Google Maps for directions, look up restaurant reviews, or scroll through TikTok and Instagram Reels for recommendations. Our phones, which usually put every answer and resource at our fingertips, suddenly became almost useless. Figuring things out on our own was frustrating, and we constantly found ourselves wishing for the quick, easy answers that something like ChatGPT could have given us.

Yet looking back, it was one of the most memorable trips of my life. Without our phones to guide us, we interacted with locals far more than we otherwise would have. We stumbled into unexpected experiences, some wonderful and others not so much, but those unpredictable moments are the ones I cherish most today. We weren't distracted by other platforms or influenced by other people's opinions about where to go or what to see. Instead, we paid attention to each other's thoughts and words, the scenery around us, and the people we encountered along the way. What initially felt like an inconvenience gradually became a source of curiosity, courage, and appreciation. Even the smallest discoveries and accomplishments felt more meaningful, because for once, they came purely from us and not from the support of others in the media.

Just like my point about the digital camera, our phones turned into nothing more than cameras on that trip. Our memories were captured raw and imperfect, without any editing or alteration, which is exactly why they feel the most pure and natural, and why I love to recall them. What this taught me is that the slow, hard way offers something the fast way takes away: the chance to figure things out ourselves, and to feel that what happened was truly ours. Maybe the technologies that support human fulfillment aren't the ones with the most answers, but the ones that know when to step back and leave room for us to be present with each other.
