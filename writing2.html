Gianna Exercises Writing 02

Digital Camera.

Writing Exercise #2

Prompt

A Creation

What is a human-made creation that brings out the best in you? Why?

With graduation coming up and one chapter of my life closing for a new one, I've found myself trying to get the most out of each moment. That's why the human-made creation that brings out the best in me is a camera. Not the one built into my iPhone, but a digital camera whose sole purpose is to capture. In my consumer behavior class, I recently learned that memory isn't the reliable, fixed record we believe it is. It shifts easily with the present and with whatever reference points we bring to it, so what's left of each moment is more like a mist, or a few crumbs. A digital camera, with its imperfect image quality and the extra steps it takes to process each photo, holds onto what my memory can't: a scene where my perspective and emotion in that moment reflects. While my memories and thoughts keep changing, the photo keeps my raw sight, in its most aesthetic form.

A friend once told me, "I think you take the best pictures of me." I didn't quite know how to respond then, but I think I understand why now. It's because I love her. I notice her laughter, her beauty, and the little expressions that make her who she is. And when I take a picture of her, that affection naturally shapes what I choose to capture. So my photos carry more than an image. They hold my perspective, my emotions and the way I interpret that moment. In that sense, the camera brings out my capacity to care and pay attention. It gives me a way to show someone how I see them, even when I can't quite put it into words.

AI can now edit or generate high-quality images with ease, but it doesn't value what I love about my camera: the shaky motion, the flash in a dark room, the textured, almost historic quality, and the faces and memories with one another that can't be changed. My iPhone takes sharper photos, but its camera is just one feature among many, and with endless options in the app, it gets in the way of the moment more than it holds it. Pulling out a digital camera is more precious as it signals to everyone that this is a "picture" moment. And the slower process of getting each photo off the camera makes me reflect on that moment once more.

Ultimately, that's what my camera has taught me. The fastest, easiest, or most technically perfect way isn't always the most meaningful. Sometimes, it's the little imperfections, the extra effort, and the quiet spaces between capturing and remembering that make an experience valuable. My camera brings out the best in me not because it helps me take better pictures, but because it reminds me to look more closely, care more deeply, and appreciate the moments and people I might otherwise take for granted.
