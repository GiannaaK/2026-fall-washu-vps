Reading Response 8.

Do Artifacts Have Ethics?

Response08 / 08
SourceL. M. Sacasas, 2014 ↗

This is a reading response to Do Artifacts Have Ethics?, L. M. Sacasas’s argument that technology is never morally neutral, along with his list of 41 questions to ask about any technology we use.

What stood out to me most in the reading was that technology doesn’t just help us do things. It also shapes how we think, behave, and experience the world. Many of Sacasas’s questions aren’t about right or wrong, but about how a technology affects our time, habits, and attention.

His questions about what technology encourages us to notice or ignore reminded me of my trip to Paris, when our phones barely worked. Without Google Maps or TikTok, we had to ask strangers for directions and make decisions ourselves. That short break showed me how much my phone usually directs my attention, and even my preferences, without my noticing. In Sacasas’s terms, it had quietly displaced the practice of exploring on our own.

As a designer, I keep coming back to his question, “What would the world be like if everyone used it exactly as I do?” For our Standby Mode project, we aren’t just designing a screen. We may be shaping how someone feels before sleep, after waking up, and throughout their day. Sacasas reminded me that good design isn’t always about making things faster or easier, but about considering what we might lose in the process.
