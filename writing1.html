Gianna Exercises Writing 01

Morning Coffee.

Writing Exercise #1

Prompt

A Ritual

What is a ritual, practice, or routine in your life that is important for your psychological wellbeing and/or fulfillment? Why?

These days, my morning ritual is getting a cup of coffee. My usual go-to cafés on campus are Café Bergson, Coffeestamp, and Corner 17, mainly because they offer free oat milk, which is always a plus for me (who is very short on meal points). Of course, where I go depends on my class schedule. If I have classes in Sam Fox, I'll stop by Coffeestamp, and if I'm heading to Simon Hall, Café Bergson it is. Corner 17, in particular, has a wide variety of coffee flavors and options, but I usually stick to my 24oz iced coffee, sometimes paired with a little sweet treat like a chocolate scone to satisfy my cravings for something sweet and creamy.

Although this daily cup of coffee might not be the healthiest habit, it helps me fully wake up, and the caffeine gives me the energy and motivation to get through the day. But if caffeine were the only reason, I could just make coffee at home. What makes this ritual special to me is everything that comes with it.

The first part is the small interactions with people. Ordering my drink, saying good morning, and having a quick exchange with the others. None of these are particularly deep conversations, and honestly, that's what I appreciate about them. These days, I often find myself caught up in complicated thoughts or conversations that require a lot of mental energy. In contrast, these brief, casual interactions give me a moment to step away from all that. There's no pressure to say something meaningful or think too deeply. It's simply a small, friendly exchange that helps me start my day feeling refreshed.

The second part is the sense of transition. Getting coffee marks the line between waking up and being ready to take on the day. Because I repeat this routine every morning, there's something reassuring about its familiarity. No matter how busy, stressful, or unpredictable the rest of my day might be, it always begins with something I know and enjoy. Even choosing oat milk is a tiny decision, but it's one that feels personal and entirely mine.

I think this ritual reminds me that fulfillment doesn't always have to come from big achievements or meaningful milestones. Sometimes, it comes from small, repeated moments that satisfy the simplest things we crave, whether that's a little sweetness, a sense of familiarity, or a brief connection with another person. For me, my morning coffee is a moment when I don't have to overthink anything. I can simply enjoy the routine, feel a little more grounded, and ease into the day.
